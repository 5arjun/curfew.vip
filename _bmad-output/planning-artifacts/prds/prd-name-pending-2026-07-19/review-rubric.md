# PRD Quality Review — Curfew (prd-name-pending-2026-07-19)

## Overall verdict

This PRD is decision-ready and unusually honest for a solo-founder build — the Phase 1/Phase 2 restructure was applied carefully and consistently across the Vision, all nine Feature groups, MVP Scope, and Success Metrics, and trade-offs (per-track hide's weak WTP signal, the subscription-vs-one-time contradiction, the parsing-validation gate) are surfaced as real tensions rather than smoothed over. The restructure left a few concrete seams, though: one Phase-1-tagged FR (FR-27) is functionally inert until Phase-2 surfaces exist, the primary paying persona's JTBD in §2.1 was never reconciled with Phase 1 only delivering half of it, and two section cross-references point at stale numbers from before the doc was reorganized. None of these are fatal, but they're the kind of thing that will confuse a downstream reader (or Arjun himself in three months) if not cleaned up before this feeds UX/architecture work.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

Trade-offs are generally named with what was given up, not just what was chosen. §4.8's Notes are a good example: "WTP survey signal... found per-track hide was the single least-wanted feature tested (6/10 wouldn't use it)... Arjun has chosen to keep both in scope regardless (now Phase 2...)" — a real decision, stated as a decision, with the counter-evidence left in view rather than deleted. §7 Monetization does the same with the subscription-vs-one-time contradiction, and §9's framing of the Phase 2 gate ("a product decision, not a calendar date") reads as an actual commitment, not a hedge.

The gap: §2.1's Jobs To Be Done for the "primary, intended paying core" persona (working club DJs) states their need as "keep me connected to my scene — seeing what my friends are playing... a community to grow with" — a JTBD that is majority-social. Phase 1 ships none of that. The tension between "this persona's core want is Phase 2" and "this persona is the intended paying core at Phase 1 launch" is real and consequential for monetization sequencing, and it is gestured at once, obliquely, in §7 ("Paying-core-vs-funnel segmentation... still needs to be reconciled... Not resolved here") — but never surfaced as a tension at the point a reader would look for it (§2.1 itself), and never given a `[NOTE FOR PM]`.

### Findings
- **high** Primary persona's JTBD is majority-Phase-2, unflagged at the point of definition (§2.1) — The club-DJ JTBD ("keep me connected to my scene," "a community to grow with") is the persona explicitly named "primary, intended paying core," but that JTBD is almost entirely Phase 2 scope. §7 references the tension in passing but §2.1 itself carries no acknowledgment that Phase 1 serves roughly half this persona's stated want. *Fix:* Add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` at §2.1 flagging that Phase 1 monetization for this persona rests on the "see how I'm evolving" half of the JTBD alone, cross-referenced to SM-2's bar and §7's unresolved segmentation question.

## Substance over theater — strong

No findings needed. The Vision (§1) is specific rather than swappable — it names real competitors and what each lacks (Serato Playlists, Songstats, DJ.Studio), cites actual WTP numbers (⅓ wouldn't use the feed, 3.0/5 invite loop), and states the moat honestly ("nobody has built the combination" vs. "nobody could build the parts"). The Cross-Cutting NFRs (§5) avoid boilerplate: Performance is grounded in "stats computation is arithmetic-only, no ML/inference required" rather than "must be fast"; Cost states a specific mechanism (no paid AI/ML API) rather than "must be cost-effective." Four personas in §2.1, each driving distinct FRs/monetization logic (club DJs → core paying core, bedroom DJs → funnel, wedding DJs → Segments FR-14/15, producer-DJs → per-track hide FR-22) — at the rubric's ceiling but not decorative.

## Strategic coherence — strong

### Findings
- **medium** SM-2 has no counter-metric — SM-1, SM-3, and SM-4 each have a matching counter-metric (SM-C1, SM-C2, SM-C3) in §10, but SM-2 ("personal value stands alone") does not. Given SM-2 gates Phase 2 alongside SM-1, an ungoverned way to "pass" it (e.g., streak-guilt notifications or dark-pattern habit loops inflating "worth opening") would undermine the thesis without being caught. *Fix:* Add an SM-C counter-metric for SM-2, e.g. "dashboard return visits must not be driven by notification pressure or gamified streak mechanics" — consistent with SM-C3's existing "no spammy notification pressure" principle.
- **high** FR-27 is tagged Phase 1 but its only stated effect requires Phase 2 surfaces — See Done-ness clarity finding below; also a coherence problem because it means one of Phase 1's launch FRs, and one of SM-1's two validation targets, has no operative behavior during the phase it's scoped to.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

About half the FRs (14 of 28) have no explicit "Consequences (testable)" subsection and rely on a single descriptive sentence — often fine (FR-12's "Aging shelf" is precisely bounded via the Glossary's 3-month definition), but a few genuinely leave "done" undefined. FR-9's "genre diversity" is used as a trend metric and again in FR-24's leaderboard, but is never defined numerically anywhere in the PRD or Glossary — an engineer has no way to know if this means a distinct-genre count, a diversity index, or something else. FR-11's "conversion rate... over a rolling window" doesn't state the window length. FR-19 ("DJ can follow other DJs") never states follow directionality (one-way vs. mutual) — it's only reconstructable by cross-referencing FR-23's "mutual follows" phrase and the Glossary's "Circle" entry.

### Findings
- **critical** FR-27's stated consequences require Phase 2 surfaces that don't exist in Phase 1, making it inert in the phase it's scoped to (§4.1, tagged *Phase 1 — Launch*) — FR-27's only described effect is gating "before the session can become visible to others (feed, comparisons, public/friends-only visibility)" — all three of those (feed, comparisons, visibility tiers) are Phase 2 features (§4.7–4.9). FR-27 itself then states "The DJ's own personal dashboard... is unaffected by this gate," explicitly ruling out any Phase 1 benefit. As written, FR-27 has zero user-visible effect during Phase 1, yet it's listed in §9.1 Phase 1 MVP Scope and is one of two FRs SM-1 (the Phase 1 blocking gate) must validate. Either FR-27 needs a stated Phase-1 purpose (e.g., protecting personal trend accuracy in FR-9/FR-11-13 from rehearsal contamination — but the FR text currently rules this out too), or it should be re-scoped: build the classification logic in Phase 1 (matches SM-1's parsing-validation intent) but move the user-facing gate itself to Phase 2 alongside the visibility surfaces it actually governs. *Fix:* Rewrite FR-27's Consequences to state what, if anything, it does in Phase 1 in isolation — or explicitly split it into "confidence scoring (Phase 1, validates parsing)" and "visibility gate (Phase 2, activates with FR-23)."
- **medium** Undefined metrics/parameters in several FRs — FR-9 "genre diversity" (no definition), FR-11 "rolling window" (no length stated), FR-19 follow directionality (inferable only via FR-23/Glossary, not stated in FR-19 itself), FR-21 "whichever aggregate stats they've chosen to make visible" (no stated mechanism for the choosing). *Fix:* Add one-line Consequences bullets or Glossary entries for each; even a placeholder `[ASSUMPTION: 90-day rolling window, TBD]` would be more decision-ready than silence.

## Scope honesty — strong

§8 Non-Goals is doing real work — ten items, each with a stated reason (e.g., skill coaching "against product identity, not just deferred" vs. Rekordbox "considered for v2"), which correctly avoids conflating true non-goals with Phase-2-deferred items (Social Feed, per-track hide, etc. are absent from §8 because they're Phase 2, not non-goals — a distinction the PRD holds cleanly). Three inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags and eight `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts sit at genuine tensions (unvalidated set-boundary detection, per-track hide's weak demand signal, unconfirmed "date added" field) rather than safe checkpoints. Open-items density (14 Open Questions + 3 Assumptions + 8 Notes) is high in absolute terms but appropriately calibrated — §10 explicitly frames this PRD as feeding "Arjun's own build rather than external stakeholders," not a green-light-to-build document for a team.

No findings beyond what's captured under Mechanical notes (Assumptions Index roundtrip gap).

## Downstream usability — adequate

Glossary is present and mostly used consistently across FRs/UJs/SMs. FR numbering has no gaps or duplicates across FR-1–FR-28 (see Mechanical notes for the non-sequential append pattern, which is benign). The main friction point is structural: §6.1's Information Architecture lists a single, unphased top-level nav — "Dashboard → Set Detail → Style Evolution → Library Utilization → Feed → Profile → Comparisons → Account/Privacy" — that silently blends Phase 1 surfaces (Dashboard through Library Utilization) with Phase 2-only surfaces (Feed, Profile, Comparisons) with no phase annotation. A UX pass sourcing nav structure directly from §6.1 would have no way to know three of eight top-level items don't exist at launch.

### Findings
- **medium** §6.1 Information Architecture doesn't phase-tag Feed/Profile/Comparisons — Every other place in the PRD that lists these three surfaces (§4.7–4.9 headers, §9.2) tags them Phase 2; §6.1 doesn't, presenting one flat nav list. *Fix:* Split §6.1 into "Phase 1 IA" and "Phase 2 additions," or inline phase tags per item, matching the discipline already used in §4 and §9.

## Shape fit — strong

Consumer product with meaningful UX — UJs are correctly load-bearing here (7 of them, each phase-tagged, each with persona+context/entry/path/climax/resolution/Realizes structure). The capability-level PRD + technical-how addendum split (§0 Document Purpose explains this explicitly) is the right call for a solo-build feeding architecture work: it keeps the PRD narrative-readable while the addendum carries parser/format specifics (triseratops licensing, `master.sqlite` vs. `database V2`, field-coverage percentages) that would bloat the PRD without adding product-decision value. The one soft spot is UJ protagonist naming — see Mechanical notes.

## Mechanical notes

- **Broken cross-reference (§0 Document Purpose, line 12)**: "inferences are tagged inline `[ASSUMPTION]` and indexed in §9" — the Assumptions Index is actually §12, not §9 (§9 is MVP Scope). Likely a stale reference from before the document was renumbered during the phase restructure.
- **Broken cross-reference (§4.1, FR-27 Notes, line 182)**: "tracked in §8 Open Questions" — Open Questions is actually §11; §8 is Non-Goals (Explicit). Same likely cause as above — section numbers shifted during restructure and this reference wasn't updated. (Contrast with the correct "§11 Open Questions" references elsewhere, e.g. §4.8 Notes, §5.2 — so the fix pattern is already established in the doc, just not applied here.)
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip fails**: three inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags exist in the PRD body (§4.4 FR-11–13 "date added" field; §7 Monetization pricing; §10 Success Metrics "no numeric targets are set for any metric above"), but §12 Assumptions Index lists only the first two. The §10 numeric-targets assumption is not indexed. *Fix:* Add a third bullet to §12 for the SM numeric-targets assumption.
- **"V1" terminology drift**: Vision §1 explicitly defines "Phase 1 (V1 Launch)," anchoring "V1" = Phase 1 for the rest of the document — and most usages respect this (FR-27/FR-28 notes, FR-8, §8 Non-Goals). One instance breaks it: FR-20's Notes (§4.7, tagged *Phase 2*) says a card format isn't "fixed... in v1," using "v1" to mean the Feed's own first release rather than product V1/Phase 1. Low-risk in context but worth tightening given how load-bearing the V1=Phase1 equivalence is elsewhere. *Fix:* Reword to "in its initial Phase 2 release" or similar.
- **UJ protagonists are role-based, not named**: all seven UJs use generic role descriptions ("A gigging club DJ," "A DJ browsing the feed," "A producer-DJ reviewing a just-synced set") rather than a named protagonist carrying context inline (e.g., "Maria, a club DJ who plays..."). Given this is a consumer product where the rubric treats named UJ protagonists as load-bearing, this is a consistent, easy, low-cost improvement — not a structural problem, since each UJ otherwise has full persona+context/entry/path/climax/resolution scaffolding.
- **FR ID ordering**: FR-1 through FR-28 have no gaps or duplicates (all 28 numbers used exactly once), but FR-26/27/28 are appended out of positional order within their sections (e.g., FR-27 sits in §4.1 after FR-1–5 but is numbered higher than everything through §4.9) — consistent with late additions during the restructure that preserved existing IDs rather than renumbering. Benign, but worth confirming this is understood by anyone doing ID-based traceability downstream (e.g., story creation).
- **Minor glossary variant**: UJ-1 uses "Layer 2 details" once where the canonical Glossary term is "Layer 2 enrichment" (§3, §4.6). Low severity, single occurrence.
