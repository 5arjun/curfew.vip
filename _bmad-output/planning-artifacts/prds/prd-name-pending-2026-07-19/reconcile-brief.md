# Reconciliation: brief.md vs. prd.md + addendum.md

Source input: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/briefs/brief-name-pending-2026-07-19/brief.md`
Compared against: `prd.md` + `addendum.md` in `prd-name-pending-2026-07-19/`

Method: read all three documents in full; checked every brief section (Executive Summary, Problem, Solution, What Makes This Different, Who This Serves, Success Criteria, Scope, Known Risks & Open Questions, Vision) against the PRD's corresponding structures (Vision, Journeys, Features/FRs, Non-Goals, Success Metrics, Open Questions, Assumptions Index) and the addendum.

Deliberate supersessions were excluded from the gap list per instructions: desktop-app → local-agent/website split, single saturated city → combined Philly/NYC/NJ, per-track hide "flagship mechanic" → "one feature among several." These are correctly and explicitly carried in the PRD (Vision §1, §6.2, §4.8 description, Open Question 5) as intentional reframes, not oversights.

## Gaps identified (things from the brief not reflected anywhere in PRD or addendum)

### 1. Competitive landscape / "why nothing else does this" narrative — entirely absent
The brief's Problem section names specific existing tools and explains why none solve the combined problem: *"Serato Playlists lets you upload a history but has no reflection layer or privacy controls; Songstats does track-level industry analytics for artists/labels, not personal reflection; DJ.Studio does prep-time library stats, not post-set reflection. Nothing combines automatic parsing, personal reflection, and scene-level privacy-aware sharing in one place."* None of these three named competitors, nor the "nothing combines all three" differentiation claim, appear anywhere in the PRD or addendum. This is real positioning language the brief cared about (it's also referenced again in "What Makes This Different" as "it sees what commercial tools can't") and it has no home in the PRD at all — not in Vision, not in an Open Question, not even as a dropped-on-purpose note.

### 2. The "honest caveat" / moat framing is lost
Brief, "What Makes This Different": *"the product's biggest advantage — the social/privacy layer — is also the piece with the weakest validated demand signal so far... The moat here is closer to 'nobody has built the combination' than 'nobody could build the parts.'"* This is a distinct strategic articulation — not just the WTP data point (which the PRD does carry forward faithfully in §4.8 Notes, §7, and Open Question 5), but the interpretive claim about *what kind of moat this even is*. The PRD retains the underlying survey numbers but never restates this framing anywhere (Vision, Open Questions, or otherwise). Worth flagging since it's exactly the kind of "why we're doing this despite weak signal" reasoning a PM revisiting the PRD later would want preserved, not just the raw data.

### 3. "Redacted banger" culture-risk open question — dropped from Open Questions
Brief's Known Risks & Open Questions lists this as its own distinct risk, separate from the WTP demand-signal risk: *"Culture risk, not yet resolved. Whether the 'redacted banger' scarcity mechanic actually drives engagement, versus just reading as frustrating, is unaddressed by any research so far — it's a design bet, not a validated finding."* The PRD's Open Questions (§11) and §4.8 Notes carry forward the WTP *demand* signal (6/10 wouldn't use per-track hide) but never carry forward this separate, more experiential question — whether the redaction mechanic, if used, actually feels engaging/scarce-and-desirable versus just annoying to the DJ or their audience. This is a genuinely distinct open question the brief explicitly named and the PRD silently dropped, not a restatement of the demand-signal risk already captured.

### 4. Dual-purpose framing of per-track hide, and the underlying cultural tension, are thinned out
Brief: per-track hide is *"a single 'per-track hide' mechanic that serves both the DJ protecting a track ID and the DJ who just wants to keep one collaborator's track private."* It's also grounded in an explicit cultural observation: *"many DJs treat their track selections as competitive IP and are protective of revealing exact tracklists, while others are fully open. A tool that assumes either extreme alienates half its potential users."* In the PRD, this is reduced to a single clause in Vision §1 ("protects the secretive DJ") and UJ-4, whose walkthrough is scoped only to a producer-DJ hiding their *own* unreleased track. The "protecting a collaborator's unreleased track" use case — and the broader "alienates half its potential users" cultural reasoning for why the mechanic needs to serve both extremes — isn't present anywhere in the PRD. This is separate from (and predates) the deliberate flagship→one-of-several downgrade; it's about which use cases the mechanic is meant to cover, which is scope-relevant, not just tone.

### 5. Implied free/paid segmentation strategy is missing
Brief, "Who This Serves," is explicit about a two-sided growth/monetization shape: working DJs are *"the primary target and intended paying core"* while bedroom DJs are *"the highest-engagement free-tier/funnel audience and word-of-mouth engine, not the initial paying customer."* The PRD's JTBD list (§2.1) keeps both segments but drops the paying-core-vs-funnel framing entirely, and §7 Monetization doesn't mention which segment is expected to convert or that bedroom DJs are the free-tier growth engine. Given monetization is already flagged as unresolved in the PRD (§7, Open Question 10), this specific piece of prior directional thinking from the brief seems to have been lost rather than deliberately deferred — nothing marks it as carried-forward-but-unresolved the way other deferred items are (e.g., §7's WTP "follow should be free" note *is* carried forward, but the paying-core/funnel segmentation isn't).

## Minor/secondary observations (not counted as core gaps, but noted for completeness)

- Brief's WTP caveat is *"n=10, convenience sample, low confidence"* and *"re-testing with a larger, **less pro-skewed** sample."* The PRD's repeated citations (§4.8, §7, Open Question 5) keep "n=10, low confidence" but drop both the "convenience sample" methodology caveat and, more substantively, the "pro-skewed" qualifier — i.e., the brief's implication that the n=10 sample over-represented experienced/pro DJs and under-represented bedroom DJs, which is relevant context for how much to trust the "per-track hide is least-wanted" signal. Worth restoring if the PRD's Open Question 5 is revisited.
- Brief's "Who This Serves" note that working DJs face *"low competition among them"* which *"makes the social layer additive rather than threatening"* — a reason the social layer is expected to work for the core segment — isn't restated in the PRD; related to gap #5 above but distinct enough to flag separately if useful.
- Not a brief-vs-PRD gap but noted in passing: the PRD references **FR-26 ("comments")** twice (UJ-2 realizes-list, §9.1 MVP Scope) but §4.7 Social Feed never actually defines an FR-26 entry alongside FR-19–21. Since "comments" isn't in the brief at all, this looks like a feature added during PRD discovery whose FR entry got missed on write-up — an internal PRD consistency issue, not a brief-reconciliation gap, but likely worth a fix pass.
- Brief's format-maintenance risk note has a specific two-part shape: *"judged low-risk due to redundancy across [multiple independent open-source] projects, but at least one (`triseratops`) explicitly warns of breaking changes over time."* The addendum lists `triseratops` as a dependency and both docs describe golden-file regression testing as mitigation, but neither restates the underlying risk calibration (why it's judged low-risk) or the source-specific caveat (triseratops itself warning of breakage). The operational mitigation survived; the risk-assessment reasoning behind it didn't.
- Brief's Vision describes a specific future B2B product: aggregated/anonymized **roster-level** data DJ agencies could use to *"differentiate their roster's playing styles to clients"* — an external, client-facing tool. PRD §2.2 Non-Users instead describes the future B2B item as *"library-overlap analysis as a team tool"* — an internally-facing tool (checking library overlap between DJs on a roster). These read as two different product ideas under the same "future B2B, not V1" heading, not just a paraphrase; worth confirming whether this was an intentional pivot during discovery or a drift in restatement.

## Things checked and confirmed NOT gaps (brief content correctly present)

- Success Criteria: all four brief bullets (parsing correctness, personal value stands alone, scene feed alive, DJ-to-DJ growth) map cleanly to SM-1–SM-4.
- Known Risks: social-vs-reflection tension, segmentation validation gap, path-join complexity, format maintenance/drift risk, and scene-critical-mass risk are all present in PRD Open Questions and/or the addendum (path-join and format-drift are in fact covered in more technical depth in the addendum than the brief had).
- Scope: every "In for V1" and "Explicitly out for V1" bullet from the brief maps to a PRD feature/FR or Non-Goal.
- Vision: the Strava analogy and "peer-driven not ad-driven" framing are both preserved verbatim in spirit in PRD Vision §1.
- The three deliberate supersessions (desktop app, single city, flagship-mechanic framing) are correctly and explicitly reframed, with the PRD calling out the correction inline (e.g., §6.2: "per explicit correction during this PRD's discovery").
