# Strava for DJs

**A mirror that tells you the truth about your DJing.** It reads your Serato history and turns every gig into personal reflection stats plus a scene-based social layer — so you can finally see how you actually play, and see how your friends play too.

## The problem

Every DJ generates rich session data every single time they play — what they mixed, when, in what key, how the room moved. Then it dies inside proprietary software, locked in Serato's files and never looked at again. No tool combines automatic history parsing, personal reflection, and privacy-first scene sharing. The data is right there. Nobody uses it.

## The solution

Effortless by design — everything is after-the-fact, derived from the files, near-zero input:

- **Reads your Serato history** once the night is over. No tagging mid-set, no gear to babysit.
- **Per-set dashboard** where every stat answers *"compared to what?"* — each number sits against your own baseline (Strava-style relative framing).
- **The room's pulse** — an energy arc of the night built from BPM over time.
- **Library utilization** — *"am I playing what I bought?"* Aging shelf, conversion rate, time-to-first-play.
- **A social feed** of your scene, where each set shows up as an energy-arc thumbnail. Scroll by the *shape* of the night, tap the ones that intrigue you, see the tracklist and stats.
- **Per-track hide** — cross out any single track in an otherwise-visible setlist. One control serves the secretive DJ hiding IDs and the collaborator hiding just the one track an artist asked to keep private.

No paid AI. Key, BPM, genre come from the file; key transitions are a Camelot-wheel lookup; the energy arc is BPM vs. time. Near-zero marginal cost.

## Why it's different

- **Reflection, not coaching.** It shows you your trajectory — widening BPM range, growing genre count, less reliance on safe tracks — it never grades your beat-matching. That's the positioning moat: experienced DJs trust a mirror, not a teacher.
- **Redacted tracks make sharing a gift.** The withheld ID is what makes the shared ones valuable. That crossed-out banger you heard in the crowd and desperately want — *it is what it is.* Scarcity turns the privacy control into the emotional engine.
- **It sees what no commercial tool can** — your unreleased edits, mashups, and self-produced tracks show up in your stats and your scene, because they're in your history whether or not they ever hit a store.

## Who pays

Working, gigging DJs who buy music and have scenes. Flat **subscription**, gated by a **~2-week free trial** — everyone trials free, then pays. Bedroom hobbyists who rip their music and have no scene are the funnel, not the customer.

## Go-to-market

Launch to **one saturated scene** — a single city, ~20 DJs in a tight club rotation — so the feed is alive on day one instead of cold and empty. Then expand city by city. Growth is **DJ-to-DJ**: *"get it so we can be friends on here and see how each other play."* The scene recruits itself.
