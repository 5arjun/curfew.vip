Brain Writing · Round Robin

passed back & forth

A sketch tossed between coach and DJ, each riff building on the last — until the shape of the product emerged: a personal reflection tool first, social second.

Coachidea

Set Rewind — a post-gig recap of a single set: the energy arc, the peak track, the biggest harmonic-risk transition, the first-time-ever played track.

DJidea

Kill the 30-second video — make Set Rewind a tap-through story baked into each set's dashboard. Treat it as a core surface, not a gimmick.

DJquestion

Does the key-transition / energy-arc calc need a paid AI API? I want to avoid AI API costs.

DJdecision

No paid AI API in the core product. Cost-averse by design.

Coachinsight

None of the core stats need AI: key/BPM/genre are read from DB metadata, key transitions = Camelot-wheel lookup, energy arc = BPM vs timestamp, first-ever = a DB query. Near-zero marginal cost.

Coachidea

‘The One Thing’ — a final card: one confident, auto-picked highlight, built to screenshot and drop into the story or group chat.

DJdecision

Tap-through Rewind is a minor share-to-story feature. The set dashboard is the core surface; the confident shareable highlight card is the keeper.

DJdecision

Spine of the dashboard: every stat answers “compared to what?” — each number shown against the DJ's own baseline. Strava-style relative framing.

DJidea

My first instinct on opening the app? Go to the friends feed, scroll to a set that looks interesting, click in — tracklist (some IDs hidden), stats, photos/videos.

DJidea

Per-set media (attach photos/videos) & leaderboards outside the feed — most sets this month, most unique tracks played.

Coachinsight

Your own first instinct is the social feed, not the personal stats — a hint that the feed is the real hook, and the personal dashboard is its supply engine.

Coachidea

Feed unit = a mini energy-arc thumbnail — the silhouette of the night. Scroll by the shape of sets; click the intriguing ones. Plus taste-based leaderboards: widest BPM range, deepest cuts, longest in-key streak, most first-plays.

DJidea

Let the DJ choose which card becomes the feed thumbnail (default = energy arc, but overridable).

DJdecision

Priority: DJ-first. A personal reflection tool primarily; social is a selling point, not the product's identity.

Role Playing

stand in their booth

Five stakeholders stepped up to the decks, each with a want, a fear, and a demand. The friction between them shaped the privacy model — and the positioning.

01 Coach

The Secretive Working DJ

Guards every ID like a trade secret.

WantReflection tools that never leak his crate.
FearHis signature IDs getting scraped and copied.
DemandPrivacy as the default — obvious, not a setting to remember. Suspects social features exist to pressure reveals.
02 DJ

The Collaborative City DJ

Same club rotations, DJ friends, low competition.

WantA shared space to see each other's sets, edits & mashups.
FearVery little — freely shares tracks with the scene.
DemandOpenness by default; only wants to hide the one track an artist asked to keep private.
03 DJ

The Wedding DJ

High-end South Asian events; repetitive sets.

WantPrompts to mix it up & actually play record-pool downloads he never explores.
FearFiller audio (family performances, dinner background) poisoning his stats.
DemandDistinct set types — sangeet, welcome, reception, mehndi — and like-to-like comparison.
04 Coach

The Bedroom DJ

Outnumbers the pros 100 : 1. Practice mixes, not sets.

WantA feedback loop — most desperate to improve, most engaged daily user.
FearFeeling like a fraud at a pro dashboard.
DemandWon't pay for “pro analytics” — but might pay to feel on a path to becoming real.
05 Coach

The DJ Company

A wedding/DJ agency with a roster to manage.

WantA roster of DJs whose libraries are differentiated, so clients can tell them apart.
FearInterchangeable DJs — too much library overlap across the team.
DemandLibrary-overlap analysis across the roster — a business tool, a potential paid company tier.
DJ · insight

Two opposite privacy poles, satisfied by the same mechanic: a per-track hide / cross-out in an otherwise-visible setlist. The secretive DJ hides all his IDs; the collaborative DJ hides only the one an artist asked him to.

DJ · decisions

Reject skill-coaching (transition quality, beat-match tightness): it isn't detectable from history files alone, and “teach DJs to improve” dilutes the product into a teaching tool. Stay a reflection tool.  •  Scope = DJs only for now; labels/artists are a future audience.  •  The bedroom DJ's “am I improving?” is served by style-evolution over time (widening BPM range, growing genre count, less reliance on safe tracks) — reflecting a trajectory, never coaching.

tell it like you've had a few

Two loose, unfiltered pitches on a bar napkin. The slurring stripped away the pitch-deck polish and revealed the real growth loop — and the emotional core.

Coach
Drunk pitch · scrawled ~ 1 a.m.
“dj to dj u gotta check this out — it reads ur Serato at the club, analyzes what ur playing, gives u every stat, i LEARNED stuff about my djing. get it so we can be FRIENDS on it and see each others stats & how we dj.”
DJ
Coach · insight

The slur revealed the true growth loop: DJs recruit each other, DJ-to-DJ — peer-to-peer within a scene, not via ads. The emotional hook is “I learned things about my djing” (self-discovery). The social value is comparing how we DJ, not competing.

Coach · build

See a buddy's track in his stats → tap it → now it's in your crate to try. The social feed doubles as a crate-digging tool; the scene “steals from each other lovingly” and everyone levels up.

his stats → tap → your crate
DJ · build

The magic is we can see each other's played tracks — established DJs give back to the people who look up to them by sharing what they play.

generosity = the whole point
◆ the emotional core ◆
The redacted track you heard in the crowd — you know it's a banger, you desperately want it —
and there it is, crossed out:
“it is what it is.”
Coach · insight

The core emotional engine is generosity / mentorship. The redacted-banger frustration is the product's central dramatic tension — the withheld track is what makes the shared ones feel valuable. Privacy controls aren't just protection: they're what gives sharing its meaning (scarcity).

DJ · design principle

Minimize user input. No in-the-moment tagging while DJing — nobody thinks about stats mid-set. Everything is after-the-fact. The app must be effortless: derive as much as possible automatically from the files.

Layer 1 · automatic

Pure file data

Works for every DJ, zero effort. This is the product — the hobbyist lives entirely here.

Layer 2 · optional tags

Enrichment

Requests, popped-off, venue, notes — richer stats only for the sets a DJ chooses to enrich. Graceful absence; tags are bonus depth, never required.

Coach · insight Effort scales with investment — the constraint “make it effortless” became the two-layer data architecture.

Convergence · the picture that assembled itself

Four things were really one

By the end, the scattered ideas snapped into a single, coherent product. Here's how they connected.

1

Three tensions were the same tension

Per-track hide + “scarcity makes sharing a gift” resolves the secretive DJ, the collaborative DJ, and the redacted banger all at once. The privacy control IS the emotional engine.

2

The constraint became the architecture

The effortless-design law and the two-layer data model are the same idea. Demanding near-zero effort is exactly what produced Layer 1 (automatic) + Layer 2 (optional).

3

The wildcard delivered the breakthroughs

Drunk History — the loosest technique — produced the real payloads: the growth loop (DJ-to-DJ), the emotional core (generosity + the redacted banger), and the social model.

4

What was cut is the moat

Saying no — reflection, not coaching; DJs only, not labels — is the positioning moat. The exclusions are what keep it sharp.

🔒 Direction locked

A Serato-first, set-as-unit reflection + social platform for DJs — effortless & after-the-fact, per-track-hide privacy, a 2-week trial → subscription, launched to one scene, then expanded city by city.

The four forks that set the direction

The Set

With optional typed segments (dancefloor / dinner / performance). Serves club DJs simply and wedding/multi-context DJs.

Social from day one

Dashboard + feed + leaderboards together. Highest ceiling; accepts the cold-start risk.

Serato first

Club / open-format base, matches the drunk pitch, known history format. Rekordbox in v2.

Subscription

Gated by a ~2-week free trial, not a permanent free tier. Everyone trials, then pays.

DJ · launch strategy

Social ships in v1, but launch is deliberately scoped to one saturated scene / city (~20 DJs in a tight rotation) so the feed is alive on day one — then expand city by city. Cold-start risk becomes the go-to-market plan.
