- (technique) started Role Playing
- (idea by coach) ROLE Secretive working DJ: wants reflection tools but fears IID leaks; DEMANDS privacy be the DEFAULT and obvious, not a setting to remember; suspicious social features exist to pressure reveals
- (idea by user) ROLE Average club DJ in tight-knit collaborative city scene: plays same club rotations w/ DJ friends, low competition, freely shares tracks; many produce own edits/mashups/tracks. WANTS a shared space to see each other's stuff.
- (insight by user) Two opposite privacy poles both satisfied by SAME mechanic: per-track hide/cross-out in an otherwise-visible setlist (secretive DJ hides IDs; collaborative DJ hides only the one track an artist asked to keep private)
- (idea by user) ROLE High-end South Asian wedding DJ: sets are repetitive, wants prompts to mix it up & actually play record-pool downloads he never explores. Has DISTINCT set types per event (sangeet, welcome, reception, mehndi) and wants to compare like-to-like (two sangeet dancefloor sets).
- (insight by user) DATA QUALITY problem: private-events DJs play non-dancefloor audio — performance tracks from family, filler between speeches, dinner background music. These would poison stats (e.g. avg song playtime). Need to exclude/segment non-set audio.
- (question by user) Multi-context DJ (weddings AND clubs): does segment differentiation happen for every set? How does the app detect when a new segment starts? (auto-detect vs manual boundary)
- (insight by coach) Segmentation must be OPTIONAL/per-user: club-only DJs never see it; multi-context DJs opt in. Detection likely: auto-guess boundaries from gaps/track-length/mixing-density, user confirms with one tap. 'Set type' is a label the DJ can assign; app learns their common types.
- (idea by coach) ROLE Bedroom DJ (100:1 vs pros): doesn't play 'sets', does practice mixes/restarts; feels like a fraud at a pro dashboard; most desperate to improve & has no feedback loop; most engaged daily user; won't pay for 'pro analytics' but might pay to feel on a path to becoming real
- (decision by user) REJECT skill-coaching features (transition quality, beat-match tightness): (1) not detectable from history files alone — would need live Serato/Rekordbox monitoring; (2) POSITIONING RISK — 'teach DJs to improve' dilutes the product and makes experienced DJs see it as a teaching tool, not a reflection tool. Stay a reflection tool.
- (decision by user) SCOPE: product is FOR DJs only for now. Labels/artists (and their tailored services/functions) are explicitly a FUTURE audience, not v1. Don't design for them yet.
- (decision by user) REFRAME keeper: bedroom-DJ 'am I improving?' need is served by existing 'style evolution over time' (BPM range widening, genre count growing, less reliance on safe tracks) — reflection of trajectory, NOT coaching. Serves the 100:1 segment without becoming a teaching app.
- (technique) started Morphological Analysis
- (idea by coach) MORPH combo 'Crew Tool': segment unit + utilization + private crews + fully private + free core/paid crew tier. Killer feature = shared 'crew crate' showing which tracks circulating among your crew you haven't played yet; sidesteps secrecy problem via trusted circles only.
- (idea by user) MORPH combo 'The Scene Tool': reads both+media, unit=the set, aha=evolution, social=public feed+leaderboards, privacy=friends-only shared, money=subscription, platform=Serato+Rekordbox+companion website. For the EXPERIENCED DJ who enjoys their own stats AND their DJ buddies' who play the same clubs; wants to be KNOWN for what they play & their style — privacy not a concern for this persona.
- (idea by coach) MORPH combo 'Digger's Ledger': library metadata only + track unit + utilization + NO social + one-time purchase. Pure solo tool answering 'I spend $200/mo on music — am I playing it?' (aging shelf, conversion, time-to-first-play). Smallest shippable wedge, not the platform.
- (idea by user) MORPH combo 'Free Hobbyist': money=free forever. Bedroom/roommate hobbyist DJ, no DJ friends so leaderboards/social useless, rips music from YouTube/SoundCloud so utilization/'am I playing what I bought' angle is moot (spent no money).
- (insight by coach) The free hobbyist breaks BOTH monetization hooks: no social (won't pay for feed/crew) AND no money spent on music (utilization/aging-shelf pitch is moot). Implication: this segment is a free-tier funnel / word-of-mouth engine, NOT a paying customer. Monetization must target working/gigging DJs who buy music and have scenes.
- (idea by user) MORPH combo 'Arsenal Audit' (unit=whole history/library): pure stats tool answering — how diverse is my arsenal really? Am I lacking music in a genre I like to play? Are other DJs playing tracks that'd mix well into my library? What do other DJs AT MY WEDDING COMPANY have in their libraries — do I need more unique tracks so clients can differentiate us?
- (insight by coach) NEW B2B/roster angle: wedding/DJ COMPANIES as a customer. A company wants its roster's libraries to be DIFFERENTIATED so clients can tell DJs apart. Library-overlap analysis across a roster = a business tool, not just a personal one — potential paid team/company tier.
- (insight by coach) Emerging monetization SPINE (3 rungs): free hobbyist (funnel) -> paid individual working DJ (utilization + social/scene) -> paid company/roster tier (library differentiation across a DJ company's roster)
- (technique) started Drunk History Retelling
- (idea by coach) DRUNK (coach): 'it's the WATCH for song wizards — Strava for DJs, but it's a mirror that's had a few and just tells you the truth about yourself. You can't lie to it.'
- (idea by user) DRUNK (user): 'dj to dj u gotta check this out — it reads ur Serato at the club, analyzes what ur playing, gives u every stat, i LEARNED stuff about my djing. get it so we can be FRIENDS on it and see each others stats & how we dj.'
- (insight by coach) The drunk pitch reveals the TRUE growth loop: DJs recruit each other DJ-to-DJ ('get it so WE can be friends & see each others stats'). The product spreads peer-to-peer within a scene, not via ads. Also confirms the core emotional hook = 'I learned things about my djing' (self-discovery), and the social value = comparing HOW we dj, not competing.
- (idea by coach) DRUNK build (coach): see a buddy's track in HIS stats -> tap it -> now it's in YOUR crate to try. The social feed doubles as a crate-digging tool; the scene 'steals from each other lovingly' & everyone levels up.
- (idea by user) DRUNK build (user): the magic is WE CAN SEE each other's played tracks — DJs give back to the community/people who look up to them by sharing what they play. Tension: the redacted track you HEARD in the crowd, know is a banger, and desperately want — 'it is what it is'.
- (insight by coach) CORE emotional engine = generosity/mentorship: established DJs sharing tracks with those who look up to them. The redacted-banger frustration is the product's central dramatic tension — the withheld track is what makes the shared ones feel valuable. Privacy controls aren't just protection, they're what gives sharing its MEANING (scarcity).
- (technique) started INVENTED #1: The Impossible Stat Machine — generate absurd/impossible stats with no feasibility filter, then mine the ridiculous ones for the real hidden hunger
- (idea by coach) IMPOSSIBLE STATS (coach): Dancefloor Retention Rate (% who stayed vs left for bar per track); Regret Index; Goosebump Moment (heart-rate spike second); Nemesis Track (the citywide banger you refuse to play out of pride); Sixth Sense Score (read the room & switched right before a drop); Track-You'll-Be-Sick-Of-In-3-Months (burnout prediction)
- (idea by user) IMPOSSIBLE STATS (user): % of crowd who put hands up when I hyped it on the mic; requests received vs requests actually played; average crowd heart rate as a line graph over the set; the track that raised dancefloor serotonin most; happiness of bride & groom based on the song playing
- (idea by coach) IMPOSSIBLE-mined features: (1) Request log = requests received vs played — SHIPPABLE v1, data no history file has; (2) rename energy arc -> 'the room's pulse'; (3) one-tap 'popped off'/'saved it' markers -> personal arsenal of proven floor-fillers & rescue tracks
- (decision by user) DESIGN PRINCIPLE: minimize user input. NO in-the-moment tagging while djing (a DJ won't think about stats mid-set). Everything is AFTER-THE-FACT. App must be effortless / near-zero-effort — derive as much as possible automatically from the files, not from manual marking. (Request-logging is borderline; even it should be after-the-fact.)
- (idea by coach) Infer-don't-ask option: derive 'did it work' from mixing behavior in the file — play-duration-vs-track-length + what the DJ reached for next (short play + energy spike after = flop + rescue). Free, no tapping.
- (decision by user) RESOLUTION on enrichment: after-the-fact OPTIONAL tagging (request, popped-off, etc — whatever the DJ wants). If they don't tag, that specific stat simply isn't tracked for that set. Graceful absence — the app still works fully on pure file data; tags are bonus depth, never required.
- (insight by coach) TWO-LAYER DATA MODEL crystallized: Layer 1 automatic (pure file data — works for every DJ, zero effort, IS the product) + Layer 2 optional tags (requests/popped-off/venue/notes — unlock richer stats only for sets the DJ enriches). Effort scales with investment; hobbyist stays L1, pro builds L2.
